<Box kind="care" title="Seek care now">

Any one of these needs care straight away. Don't wait to see if it settles, and don't wait for your urine to change.

- Muscle pain far worse than you'd expect from the exercise you did, or that keeps getting worse.
- Marked swelling of a muscle, or a muscle that feels tight and hard.
- Muscle weakness, such as struggling to stand up from a chair or climb stairs.
- Dark urine, the colour of tea or cola, or passing much less urine than usual.

Call emergency services for confusion, a seizure, or collapse.

## Normal soreness

After a new kind of exercise, muscles often ache. This delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) usually starts within a day, is worst around one to three days after, and fades within about a week<Cite id="S18" />. Your muscles feel stiff and tender, and the first steps of the day or going down stairs are the worst part.

It's worst after exercise in which muscles work while they lengthen, like running downhill or the braking part of each step<Cite id="S19" />. So a hilly first run, or a first run that's longer than you're used to, tends to leave you sorer.

Better news: after one bout, doing the same exercise again causes much less soreness and muscle damage. This is called the repeated bout effect<Cite id="S20" />, and it's why the first week or two of running are usually the sorest.

## What helps

- **Keep moving gently.** Walking or easy cycling often makes sore muscles feel better.
- **Keep the next run easy,** or take an extra rest day if you're very sore.
- **Build up gradually.** Smaller steps mean less soreness. <TopicLink to="start-running/how-often-how-far">How often and how far</TopicLink> has the details.

Being sore doesn't mean a run was good, and not being sore doesn't mean it was wasted.

## Pain to get checked

Normal soreness is a general ache in the muscles, on both sides, that eases as you warm up. Some patterns are different, and are worth stopping running for and getting checked by a doctor or physiotherapist:

- sharp pain in one spot, or pain that gets worse as you keep running;
- pain that makes you limp, or changes how you run;
- pain in a bone that hurts to press, or that aches at night or at rest;
- swelling around a joint or tendon;
- pain that hasn't improved after a week.

You can't always tell these apart yourself, and you don't need to. A clinician can examine you and decide whether you need tests. <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">Soreness or pain?</TopicLink> goes further.

## About rhabdomyolysis

Very hard or unaccustomed exercise can break down so much muscle that its contents leak into the blood and can harm the kidneys. This is exertional rhabdomyolysis. Its warning signs include muscle pain or weakness out of proportion to the exercise, swelling, and tea- or cola-coloured urine<Cite id="S21" />. It's diagnosed by clinicians from your symptoms together with blood tests, which may need repeating, and the treatment depends on how severe it is.

Heat and being dehydrated can make it worse. Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether your medicines or supplements change your risk.

It's listed here so that you know the warning signs, and the box at the top of this page says what to do.
