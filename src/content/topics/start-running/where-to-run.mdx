## Roads and pavements

Most runners start from their front door, on pavements and quiet roads. They're convenient, easy to measure, and usually lit.

People worry that hard surfaces jar the body. Your legs are better at this than you might think: runners adjust the stiffness of their legs to the surface, running with softer legs on hard ground and stiffer legs on soft ground, so the body bounces about the same on both<Cite id="S15" />. <TopicLink to="how-running-works/springs-and-tendons">Springs and tendons</TopicLink> explains how the leg works as a spring.

Where there is no pavement, run facing the oncoming traffic, so you can see it and step aside. Take extra care at junctions and driveways, and assume drivers haven't seen you.

## Trails and grass

Park paths, grass and trails are softer, quieter and usually prettier. They're also uneven, so your ankles and feet work harder and your pace will be slower for the same effort. Run them by effort, not pace, and watch your footing, especially on roots, wet rock and downhill sections.

On trails, take a phone and tell someone your route, as you may be far from help.

## Running tracks

A standard outdoor track is 400 metres around the inside lane. Tracks are flat, measured and traffic-free, which makes them ideal for faster sessions later on. For easy running, use the outer lanes and leave the inside lanes to faster runners, and follow the posted direction.

## Treadmills

A treadmill is a perfectly good place to start, especially in the dark, the heat or the cold. A review of studies comparing the two found treadmill running broadly similar to running overground in how people move, with some differences<Cite id="S14" />.

It's not quite the same effort, though. There's no air resistance and the belt helps a little. For trained runners at moderate to fast speeds, a 1% incline best matched the energy cost of running outdoors<Cite id="S13" />. At easy jogging speeds the difference is small.

- **Clip on the safety cord,** and start the belt slowly before you step on.
- **Look ahead,** not down at your feet.
- **Expect it to feel different** at first. Many people find the first few treadmill runs awkward; it passes.

## Staying safe

- Run in daylight or on lit routes where there are other people about, especially when you're starting.
- Vary your route and time if you often run alone.
- Trust your instincts: if somewhere feels wrong, turn round.
- Running with a friend, a group or a club is safer, and often more fun.

<Box kind="try">

Plan two or three routes of different lengths from your door: a 20-minute loop, a 30-minute loop and a longer one. On days when motivation is low, you won't have to decide where to go.
