Stop running and seek care straight away if you get pain, pressure or tightness in your chest, neck, jaw or arms; breathlessness that doesn't settle when you stop; dizziness or faintness; or a racing or irregular heartbeat. For chest pain, collapse or confusion, call emergency services.

## Most people can start gently

Starting with walking and run-walk is a gentle way in. Screening advice from the American College of Sports Medicine says that people who are inactive now, but have no known heart, metabolic or kidney disease and no symptoms of one, can start light to moderate exercise without seeing a doctor first, and build up gradually. Anyone with symptoms should get medical clearance before starting<Cite id="S24" />.

## Symptoms that mean see a doctor first

See a doctor before you start running if you have any of these, even if they come and go<Cite id="S25" />:

- pain or discomfort in your chest, neck, jaw or arms, at rest or when you're active;
- breathlessness at rest or with mild effort, more than you'd expect;
- dizziness, or fainting;
- swollen ankles;
- your heart racing, fluttering or skipping beats;
- pain in your calves when you walk that eases when you stop;
- a heart murmur you've been told about;
- unusual tiredness or breathlessness with everyday activities.

## Conditions worth a check

It's also worth talking to your doctor, nurse or midwife about how to start if you:

- have a heart condition, high blood pressure, diabetes or kidney disease;
- take regular medicine for a long-term condition;
- are pregnant or have recently given birth (see <TopicLink to="different-runners/womens-health" />);
- have a bone, joint or muscle problem that more activity could make worse;
- have been told by a doctor to be careful with exercise.

Many people with these conditions run. A check helps you start in a way that suits you, and know what to watch for.

## Questionnaires

The checklist above is a simple version of the screening questionnaires that clinicians and gyms use. The PAR-Q+ is a free questionnaire, with follow-up questions, for deciding who should get advice before becoming more physically active<Cite id="S26" />.

## What to ask your doctor

- Is it OK for me to start with walking and run-walk, and build up gradually?
- Is there anything I should avoid, or any symptoms I should watch for?
- Do any of my medicines affect exercise, for example my heart rate, blood sugar or how I handle heat?
- Should I have any tests first?

## After an illness

After a cold or other illness, wait until you've recovered before you run again, and start back more gently than before. After an illness with a fever, chest symptoms, or a long recovery, ask your doctor before returning.
