## The test

While you run, say a sentence out loud, or chat with whoever you are running with.

- **Full sentences, comfortably:** you are running easily. This is where most of your running belongs.
- **Short sentences, with pauses for breath:** you are at a steady, moderate effort.
- **A few words at a time:** you are working hard.
- **No words at all:** you are close to your limit, which you can only hold for a few minutes or less.

That's the whole test. It works on any route, in any weather, without a watch.

## Why easy is easier than you think

Most people who start running go too fast. Running feels like it should be hard, so they push, run out of breath in a few minutes, and decide they're "not a runner". Usually they were just going too quickly.

An easy run should feel almost too slow. If that means a pace barely faster than a brisk walk, or walking breaks, that's fine. You are still building your heart, lungs, muscles and tendons, and you will recover in time for the next run.

This isn't only advice for beginners. Well-trained endurance athletes in several sports do about 80% of their training sessions at low intensity<Cite id="S9" />. <TopicLink to="training/intensity-mix">Easy running and the intensity mix</TopicLink> explains why.

## What the talk test measures

As effort rises, you breathe harder. At some point your breathing speeds up sharply: it's called the ventilatory threshold, and it marks the step from moderate to hard exercise. In lab tests, the point where talking stopped being comfortable sat close to it<Cite id="S7" />. So the simple question "can I still chat?" lines up well with a measurement that usually needs a breathing mask.

Scientists also use scales of perceived exertion, where you rate how hard exercise feels. The best known, Borg's scale, runs from 6 (no exertion at all) to 20 (maximal exertion)<Cite id="S8" />. The dial above uses a simpler 1 to 10.

## Using it on real runs

- **Hills:** slow down, or walk, so you can still talk. The effort is what matters, not the pace.
- **Heat, wind and tiredness:** the same pace feels harder. Keep the effort easy and let the pace drop.
- **On your own:** say a sentence out loud now and then. Counting to ten works too.
- **With a heart-rate monitor:** <TopicLink to="the-engine/heart-rate-zones">heart-rate zones</TopicLink> give a number to the same idea. The talk test is a good way to check that your zones are set sensibly.

<Box kind="care" title="When breathlessness is not normal">

Getting out of breath when you run fast is normal, and it settles within a minute or two of slowing down. Stop and seek care straight away if breathlessness doesn't settle when you stop, or comes with chest pain, tightness, faintness or a racing or irregular heartbeat. For chest pain, collapse or confusion, call emergency services.
