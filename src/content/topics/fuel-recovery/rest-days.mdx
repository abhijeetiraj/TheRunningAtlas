## Why rest makes you fitter

A hard run doesn't make you fitter on the day. It tires you out and leaves small amounts of damage. It's in the hours and days afterwards, as your body repairs and adapts, that you get fitter.

A joint consensus statement from the European College of Sport Science and the American College of Sports Medicine describes it as a balance. Training improves fitness when hard work is matched with enough recovery. A short spell of overload followed by recovery can lead to improvement. When recovery falls short for long, performance can stay down for weeks or months, which is called non-functional overreaching, or longer still in the overtraining syndrome<Cite id="F12" />.

## How many rest days

There's no single right number. Some common patterns:

- **New runners**: run every other day, with rest or walking in between. Beginner plans such as Couch to 5K use three runs a week<Cite id="S1" />.
- **Regular runners**: at least one full rest day a week, with easy days between harder ones.
- **Experienced high-mileage runners**: some run most days, using very easy runs and lighter weeks for recovery.

If you're unsure, take more rest rather than less.

## What to do on a rest day

- **Nothing**, if you like. That's allowed.
- **Gentle movement**: walking, easy cycling, swimming or yoga, kept truly easy. See <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink>.
- **Strength work**, if it's light and not before a hard session. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">strength training for runners</TopicLink>.
- **Eat and sleep well.** See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/everyday-eating">everyday eating</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/sleep">sleep</TopicLink>.

## Signs you need more rest

Take a few easy or rest days if you notice:

- Your easy pace feels harder, or is slower at the same effort.
- Your legs stay heavy or sore from run to run.
- You're sleeping badly, feel irritable or have lost interest in running.
- You keep picking up small aches and niggles.

If a few easy days don't help, ease off for longer. Overreaching shows up as performance that stays down for weeks, even with rest<Cite id="F12" />.

## Easy days are recovery too

Recovery isn't only rest days. Keeping easy runs truly easy, at a chatty effort, lets you recover while still running. See <TopicLink to="training/intensity-mix">the intensity mix</TopicLink>.

<Box kind="care">

See a doctor if you feel tired for more than a couple of weeks despite resting, or if tiredness comes with weight loss, breathlessness, a racing heart or feeling faint. Tiredness can have causes other than training, and some need treatment. If you feel faint, have chest pain or unusual breathlessness while running, stop and seek care now.
