## Even effort

Pacing is how you share out your effort over a race. The usual aim is to finish having used it all, without a stretch where you've run out.

On a flat course, steady effort and steady pace are nearly the same thing, so even splits are a good target. The <TopicLink to="tools/pace-calculator">pace calculator</TopicLink> works them out.

On hills, they come apart. Running uphill costs much more energy per kilometre, and gentle downhills cost less<Cite id="H17" />. To keep your effort steady, you slow down on the climbs and let the descents give some of it back. The fly-over above shows a plan built that way. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/hills">hills</TopicLink>.

## The first kilometres

At the start, you're rested, excited and surrounded by other runners, so a fast pace feels easy. The cost comes later.

In about 92,000 performances in 14 US marathons, men slowed more than women in the second half. Men were about three times as likely to slow down a lot, running the second half more than 30% slower than the first, even allowing for age, experience and finishing time<Cite id="C1" />.

Some ways to hold back early:

- **Start at your target pace or a little slower**, and check your first few splits.
- **Run your own race.** It's easy to get pulled along by faster runners near you.
- **Save any push for the second half**, once you know how you feel.

## Choosing a target

A recent race is the best guide to what you can run. The <TopicLink to="tools/race-time-estimator">race-time estimator</TopicLink> turns one race into predictions for others, using the Riegel formula<Cite id="P3" />.

Be cautious with the marathon. In a survey of recreational runners, the formula predicted marathon times that were too fast, by at least 10 minutes for half of the runners<Cite id="P4" />. If your marathon prediction comes from a shorter race, it's sensible to aim a little slower.

Heat slows everyone. In big-city marathon results, finishing times slowed as heat stress rose, and slower runners were slowed more<Cite id="C2" />. On a warm day, adjust your target before you start. See <TopicLink to="racing/course-weather">course and weather</TopicLink>.

## During the race

- **Check effort as well as pace.** If your breathing and legs say the pace is too hard, they're usually right. See <TopicLink to="start-running/talk-test">the talk test</TopicLink>.
- **Use the kilometre or mile markers** as well as your watch. GPS can drift, especially among tall buildings and trees.
- **Shelter from a headwind** behind other runners when you can.
- **Expect the last part to feel hard.** That's normal in a well-paced race.
