## The taper

Training makes you fitter, but it also leaves you a little tired. A taper lets the tiredness clear while you keep the fitness.

Pooling studies of tapering, the biggest performance gains came from a taper of about two weeks in which training volume was cut by roughly 41 to 60%, while intensity and frequency were kept<Cite id="T4" />.

In practice:

- **Run fewer kilometres**, by shortening runs more than by dropping them.
- **Keep a little faster running**, such as a few short efforts at race pace, so your legs stay sharp.
- **Keep your usual rhythm** of running days, more or less.
- **Match it to the race.** As a rough guide from coaching practice, about a week suits a 5K or 10K, 1 to 2 weeks a half marathon and about 2 weeks a marathon<Cite id="T8" />.

Stopping or cutting training a lot for a few weeks does bring a fall in fitness<Cite id="T5" />. A taper is different: it's short, and it keeps some running at intensity, as in the studies above.

It's common to feel restless, sluggish or a bit off during a taper. That's not a sign it isn't working.

## Food in the last days

For races longer than about 90 minutes, a joint position statement suggests carbohydrate loading: about 10 to 12 g of carbohydrate per kg of body mass a day for the 36 to 48 hours before<Cite id="C3" />. For a 70 kg runner, that's about 700 to 840 g a day. See <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink>.

For a 5K or 10K, your normal meals are enough.

## Sleep

Sleep experts recommend that adults sleep 7 hours or more a night<Cite id="F9" />. Bank good nights in race week. A restless night just before the race can happen, so don't let it worry you. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/sleep">sleep</TopicLink>.

## Race-week checklist

- **Kit**: lay out everything the night before: shoes you've run in, clothes you've tested on a long run, race number, pins, watch, and anything for the weather.
- **Fuel**: the gels or drinks you've practised with, and your race breakfast. See the <TopicLink to="tools/fuel-planner">fuel planner</TopicLink>.
- **Plan**: your target pace, how you'll start, and where the aid stations are. See <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">pacing</TopicLink>.
- **Logistics**: how you'll get there, when you need to arrive, bag drop and toilets.
- **Weather**: check the forecast and adjust your plan and clothes. See <TopicLink to="racing/course-weather">course and weather</TopicLink>.

<Box kind="try" title="Nothing new on race day">

New shoes, a new breakfast or a new gel can all go wrong over a long race. Use only what you've tried in training, ideally on a long run.

</Box>

## Race morning

Eat your usual pre-run breakfast, giving yourself time to digest. Warm up with a few minutes of easy jogging and some <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/warm-up">warm-up moves</TopicLink>, more for a short race and less for a marathon. Then start steady.
