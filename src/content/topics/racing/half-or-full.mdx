## How the training differs

These are typical ranges from coaching practice, for runners who can already run comfortably for 30 minutes. They're illustrations, not tested prescriptions<Cite id="T8" />.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Half marathon | Marathon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plan length** | 10 to 14 weeks | 16 to 20 weeks |
| **Runs a week** | 4 to 5 | 4 to 6 |
| **Longest run** | 16 to 20 km | 28 to 35 km |
| **Taper** | 1 to 2 weeks | About 2 weeks |

</div>

The biggest change is the long run. At 6:00 per km, a 32 km long run takes about 3 hours 12 minutes. See <TopicLink to="training/plans">plans from 5K to marathon</TopicLink>.

## Fuel

At 6:00 per km, a half marathon takes about 2 hours 7 minutes and a marathon about 4 hours 13. Suggested carbohydrate during exercise rises with its length: up to about 30 g an hour for 1 to 2 hours, up to about 60 g for 2 to 3 hours, and up to about 90 g an hour beyond about 2.5 hours<Cite id="F4" />.

So in a marathon, eating and drinking on the move becomes part of the race, and something to practise in training. See <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink> and the <TopicLink to="tools/fuel-planner">fuel planner</TopicLink>.

## Pacing and predictions

The longer the race, the more a fast start costs. Race-time formulas can help you choose a target, but in a survey of recreational runners, the Riegel formula predicted marathon times that were too fast, by at least 10 minutes for half of the runners<Cite id="P4" />. See <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">pacing</TopicLink>.

## How to choose

- **Time**: can you fit 4 to 6 runs a week, including a long run of up to 3 hours or more, for 4 to 5 months?
- **Experience**: a half marathon first teaches you race-day pacing and fuel with less at stake.
- **Your body**: if you're prone to injury when your running goes up, a half may be the kinder goal for now. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.
- **What you want from it**: a half can be raced hard. A first marathon is often about finishing well.

<Box kind="note" title="Not a lesser goal">

A half marathon is a big achievement on its own, and you don't have to move up to the marathon at all.

</Box>
