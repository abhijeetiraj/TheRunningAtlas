<Box kind="care" title="Heat illness">

**Call emergency services** if a runner is confused, behaving oddly or collapses in the heat. Heat stroke is a medical emergency. Cooling quickly is the priority: medical teams cool the person in cold water straight away<Cite id="E33" />. While you wait, move them into the shade and cool them with whatever you have, such as cold water, ice or wet towels.

**Stop running, get out of the heat, cool down and seek care now** if you feel faint or dizzy, weak, sick, or have a headache or vomiting<Cite id="E44" />. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/red-flags">red flags</TopicLink>.

</Box>

## Heat

Heat is hard on runners because your body has to send blood to the skin to lose heat, as well as to your muscles. In results from several big-city marathons over many years, finishing times slowed progressively as heat stress rose from about 5 to 25 °C, and slower runners were slowed more than the fastest<Cite id="C2" />.

On a warm race day:

- **Slow your target pace** before you start, and run by effort. See <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">pacing</TopicLink>.
- **Drink to thirst**, which will be more than usual<Cite id="C4" />.
- **Pour water over yourself** at aid stations, and wear light, loose clothes and a cap.
- **Stop if you feel unwell.** A finish time is never worth heat illness.

Exercising in the heat before you're used to it raises the risk of heat illness<Cite id="E44" />. Heat acclimation brings earlier and greater sweating and a lower heart rate and body temperature during exercise, with much of it in the first week or so<Cite id="E31" />. If your race is likely to be hot, some training in similar conditions in the weeks before helps. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/heat-and-sweat">heat and sweat</TopicLink>.

## Cold and rain

Cold is mostly about comfort and staying warm before the start and after the finish:

- **Keep warm at the start** with an old top or a bin bag you can throw away once you're moving.
- **Wear gloves and a hat**, which are easy to take off and carry.
- **Plan for the finish**: you cool down fast once you stop, especially if you're wet. Have dry clothes ready.

Rain makes chafing and blisters more likely. Use anti-chafe balm, and wear socks and clothes you've already run in when wet.

## Wind

A headwind slows you, and a tailwind doesn't give all of it back. Run by effort into the wind, tuck in behind other runners where you can, and make up some time when it's behind you.

## Hills

Climbs cost much more energy than descents give back<Cite id="H17" />. Run the hills by effort: slower going up, relaxed and quick going down. The <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">course fly-over</TopicLink> shows a plan built that way, and <TopicLink to="how-running-works/hills">hills</TopicLink> explains why.

## Altitude

At altitude, there's less oxygen in each breath, so the same pace feels harder. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/altitude">altitude</TopicLink>.
