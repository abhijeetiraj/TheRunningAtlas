## Distance and time to train

Start with what you can already run, and how many weeks you have. As a rough guide from coaching practice, plans run from about 6 to 8 weeks for a 5K to about 16 to 20 weeks for a marathon, for runners who can already run comfortably for 30 minutes<Cite id="T8" />. See <TopicLink to="training/plans">plans from 5K to marathon</TopicLink>.

If you're not sure between two distances, see <TopicLink to="racing/half-or-full">half or full?</TopicLink>

## Date and weather

Look at the weather the race usually gets, not just the date. In results from several big-city marathons over many years, finishing times slowed progressively as heat stress rose from about 5 to 25 °C, and slower runners were slowed more than the fastest<Cite id="C2" />.

So a cool-season race usually gives you a better chance of a good time. A summer race can still be a great day out, as long as you adjust your pace and plan for the heat. See <TopicLink to="racing/course-weather">course and weather</TopicLink>.

Think about your training too. A spring race means training through winter; an autumn race means long runs in summer.

## Flat or hilly

Course profiles are usually on the race website. A flat course suits a fast time. A hilly one is slower for the same effort, because climbs cost much more energy than descents give back<Cite id="H17" />. The <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">course fly-over</TopicLink> shows how a pacing plan changes over hills.

If your race is hilly, include some hills in training. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/hills">hills</TopicLink>.

## Big or small

- **Big city races** have crowds, closed roads, pacers and lots of aid stations. They can also mean early starts, long queues and crowded first kilometres.
- **Small local races** are easier to get to and less stressful, and a good way to try racing for the first time.
- **Trail races** are slower and more varied, and need different shoes and skills.

## The practical details

- **Start time**: early starts mean an early breakfast and getting there in good time.
- **Cut-off time**: many races close the course after a set time. Check that your likely finish is well inside it.
- **Aid stations**: how often there's water, and whether there's food or sports drink.
- **Travel**: how you'll get there and back, and whether you'll need to stay the night before.
- **Cost and refunds**: what happens if you're injured or ill.
