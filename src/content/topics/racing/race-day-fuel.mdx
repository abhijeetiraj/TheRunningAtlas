## The days before

For races longer than about 90 minutes, such as a half marathon for many runners and any marathon, a joint position statement suggests carbohydrate loading: about 10 to 12 g of carbohydrate per kg of body mass a day for the 36 to 48 hours before<Cite id="C3" />.

| Body mass | Carbohydrate a day |
| --- | --- |
| 55 kg | 550 to 660 g |
| 70 kg | 700 to 840 g |
| 85 kg | 850 to 1,020 g |

That's a lot more than usual, so it means swapping some of your plate for starchy foods and sweet drinks, not just eating bigger meals. Choose foods you know agree with you. For a 5K or 10K, your normal meals are enough.

## Race breakfast

Before exercise lasting more than an hour, the same statement suggests 1 to 4 g of carbohydrate per kg of body mass, eaten 1 to 4 hours before<Cite id="F3" />. The bigger amounts suit an earlier breakfast; a smaller snack suits the last hour or two.

Eat what you've eaten before long training runs. Race morning is not the time to try something new.

## During the race

Suggested carbohydrate during exercise rises with its length<Cite id="F4" />:

- **Under about an hour**: little or none is needed.
- **1 to 2 hours**: up to about 30 g an hour.
- **2 to 3 hours**: up to about 60 g an hour.
- **Beyond about 2.5 hours**: up to about 90 g an hour, using a mix of glucose and fructose.

The <TopicLink to="tools/fuel-planner">fuel planner</TopicLink> turns those into how many gels or drinks you need, and when to take each one.

Your gut adapts to what it gets, so practise eating and drinking on long runs, at race pace for some of it. Practising may reduce stomach problems on the day, though few studies have tested this directly<Cite id="F5" />. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/before-during">before and during runs</TopicLink>.

## Drinking

Drink to thirst. The consensus statement on low blood sodium advises using thirst to guide how much you drink, because drinking more than you lose, over hours, is the main cause of exercise-associated hyponatraemia<Cite id="C4" />.

So there's no need to drink at every aid station if you're not thirsty, or to force down a set amount. In the heat, you'll be thirstier and drink more. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/fluids-sodium">fluids and sodium</TopicLink>.

<Box kind="try" title="A rehearsal">

Do one or two long runs as a full rehearsal: same breakfast, same timing, same gels or drinks, same kit. Note what worked, and keep it for race day.

</Box>
