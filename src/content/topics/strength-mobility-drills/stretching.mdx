## What stretching does

Regular stretching increases your range of motion: how far a joint can move. So does strength training through a full range. Pooling 11 trials with 452 people, the two improved range of motion by similar amounts, although the trials differed a lot<Cite id="M3" />.

Whether more range of motion makes you a better runner is less clear. But if a joint feels stiff in daily life, or a physiotherapist has found a specific restriction, stretching is one way to work on it.

## Injuries

Pooling 25 trials of more than 26,000 people, stretching programmes showed no clear protective effect against sports injuries. Strength training programmes were linked with the biggest drop<Cite id="W5" />.

Studies of stretching before activity tell the same story. In more than 1,500 army recruits, adding short static stretches to the warm-up made no meaningful difference to injury risk<Cite id="W1" />. In more than 2,700 runners, a short stretch before running neither prevented nor caused injury<Cite id="W2" />.

## Soreness

A Cochrane review of randomised trials found that stretching before, after, or before and after exercise did not produce clinically important reductions in the muscle soreness of the following days. Stretching after exercise lowered soreness a day later by about 1 point on a 100-point scale<Cite id="M6" />.

Soreness fades by itself, and your muscles adapt so the same session hurts less next time<Cite id="H25" />. See <TopicLink to="start-running/soreness">soreness</TopicLink>.

## Before or after?

Long static holds just before exercise can slightly blunt performance. Holds of 60 seconds or more per muscle cut performance by about 4.6% in tests soon after, shorter holds by about 1.1%, while dynamic stretching gave about +1.3%<Cite id="W3" />.

So before a run, a moving warm-up is the better choice. See the <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/warm-up">warm-up studio</TopicLink>. Save static stretching for after a run, or another time of day, when you're warm and in no hurry.

## How to stretch

The American College of Sports Medicine advises flexibility exercises for the major muscle groups on at least 2 to 3 days a week. Hold each static stretch for 10 to 30 seconds, to the point of tightness or slight discomfort, for about 60 seconds in total per stretch. Daily stretching gives bigger gains<Cite id="M8" />.

- Stretch when you're warm, after a run or a shower.
- Ease in slowly and breathe. No bouncing.
- Tightness is fine; pain isn't.

## Six stretches for runners

| Stretch | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Calf, straight knee** | Hands on a wall, one leg back with the heel down and knee straight. Lean in until you feel the calf. |
| **Calf, bent knee** | As above, then bend the back knee, keeping the heel down. You'll feel it lower, near the Achilles. |
| **Front of thigh** | Standing, hold one ankle behind you, knees together, hips pushed slightly forward. Hold on to something for balance. |
| **Back of thigh** | Heel on a low step, leg straight, and hinge forward from the hips with a straight back. |
| **Front of hip** | Kneel on one knee, the other foot in front. Tuck your pelvis under and shift forward gently. |
| **Seat** | Lying on your back, cross one ankle over the other knee and pull the other thigh towards you. |

<Box kind="note" title="If you like it, keep it">

Many people find stretching after a run relaxing. If it helps you wind down, or you notice you move more easily, there's no reason to stop. Just don't skip strength work in its place. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">strength training for runners</TopicLink>.
