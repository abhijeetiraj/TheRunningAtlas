## Two kinds of drill

**Technique drills** break the running stride into pieces and exaggerate them: lifting the knee, pulling the heel up, landing under you. They're done at low speed, over 15 to 30 metres.

**Springy drills**, or plyometrics, are about bouncing: quick, stiff little hops and skips that use the stretch and recoil of your tendons. They train the springy stiffness of your legs, which a review proposed helps running performance<Cite id="H4" />. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/springs-and-tendons">springs and tendons</TopicLink>.

## What the evidence says

The springy kind has the better evidence. Pooling 16 studies of endurance athletes, adding explosive training to endurance training improved running economy by about 4.8%, and heavy weight training by about 3.7%. Longer programmes went with bigger gains<Cite id="M7" />. A separate review of strength training in runners found similar gains in economy, of about 2 to 8%<Cite id="M1" />. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/running-economy">running economy</TopicLink>.

Technique drills are a different story. They're a long-standing coaching tradition, and they make a good warm-up, but we haven't found good trials showing that they change how people run on their own. Treat them as practice and preparation, not as a fix for your form.

## Technique drills

Do each one over 15 to 30 metres, walk back, and repeat two or three times. Stay tall and relaxed.

| Drill | How | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Marching A** | Walk tall, lifting one knee to hip height, toes up, opposite arm forward | Posture and knee lift, slowly |
| **A-skip** | Skip, driving one knee up with each skip, landing under your hip | Rhythm and a quick, light landing |
| **B-skip** | As the A-skip, but kick the lower leg out, then sweep it down and back under you | Pawing the ground back, coordination |
| **High knees** | Quick, short steps with knees coming up in front, staying on the balls of your feet | Quick feet, knee lift |
| **Butt kicks** | Quick, short steps pulling the heels up towards your seat | Heel recovery, quick feet |
| **Carioca** | Sideways, crossing one leg in front and then behind the other | Hip rotation and coordination |

The warm-up studio shows similar moving warm-ups in 3D. See the <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/warm-up">warm-up studio</TopicLink>.

## Springy drills

These are harder on your legs than they look, especially on your calves and Achilles tendons. Count foot contacts, and start low.

| Drill | How | A starting dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pogo hops** | Small, quick two-footed hops on the balls of your feet, knees nearly straight, springing off the ground | 2 × 15 to 20 contacts |
| **Skipping for height** | Skip, driving up for height with the knee and opposite arm | 2 × 15 metres |
| **Ankle bounces on one leg** | As pogo hops, on one leg, small and quick | 2 × 10 per leg |
| **Bounding** | Long, springy running strides, staying in the air longer than usual | 2 × 20 metres, once you're used to the rest |

These doses are common starting points from coaching practice, not tested prescriptions<Cite id="M5" />.

## How to start

- **Build a base first.** A few weeks of calf raises, squats and single-leg work prepare your tendons for hopping. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">strength training for runners</TopicLink>.
- **Soft, flat surface.** Grass, a track or a gym floor, in running shoes.
- **Fresh legs.** After a warm-up or an easy run, not after a hard session.
- **Little and often.** Once or twice a week, with a day or more in between. Add a few contacts at a time.
- **Quality over quantity.** Quick, light contacts. Stop the set when they get slow or heavy.

Strides, short relaxed runs at near your fastest smooth pace, are the gentlest way to bring speed and spring into your running. See <TopicLink to="training/workouts">workouts explained</TopicLink>.

<Box kind="care">

Hopping loads your Achilles tendons and calves a lot. Stop if you feel pain in the Achilles, the heel, the shin or the foot, and get it checked if it's there the next day. If you've had a tendon problem or a bone stress injury, ask a physiotherapist before you start. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/achilles-pain">Achilles pain</TopicLink>.
