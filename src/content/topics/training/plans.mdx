## What every plan shares

Good plans for any distance are built from the same parts:

- **Mostly easy running.** Well-trained endurance athletes do about four in five sessions at low intensity<Cite id="S9" />. See <TopicLink to="training/intensity-mix">the intensity mix</TopicLink>.
- **One or two harder sessions a week.** Tempo runs, intervals or hills. See <TopicLink to="training/workouts">workouts explained</TopicLink>.
- **A long run** that grows over the plan.
- **Gradual build-up**, with an easier week every few weeks. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.
- **A taper** before the race.

If you're new to running, start with a beginner plan first. Plans such as the NHS Couch to 5K build from short runs between walks to about 30 minutes of running over nine weeks, with three runs a week<Cite id="S1" />. See <TopicLink to="start-running/first-run">your first run</TopicLink>.

## How plans change with distance

These are typical ranges from coaching practice, for runners who can already run comfortably for at least 30 minutes. They're illustrations, not tested prescriptions<Cite id="T8" />.

<div class="table-scroll">

| | 5K | 10K | Half marathon | Marathon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plan length** | 6 to 8 weeks | 8 to 10 weeks | 10 to 14 weeks | 16 to 20 weeks |
| **Runs a week** | 3 to 4 | 3 to 5 | 4 to 5 | 4 to 6 |
| **Longest run** | 6 to 8 km | 10 to 14 km | 16 to 20 km | 28 to 35 km |
| **Main workouts** | Intervals, strides | Intervals, tempo | Tempo, long runs | Long runs, steady running |
| **Taper** | About a week | About a week | 1 to 2 weeks | About 2 weeks |

</div>

Shorter races are run closer to your top aerobic effort, so plans lean on faster intervals. The longer the race, the more it depends on endurance, fuel and economy, so plans lean on the long run and steady running. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/vo2max">VO2max</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="the-engine/carbs-and-fat">carbs and fat</TopicLink>.

## Example weeks

Here's what a middle week of each plan might look like. Shorten everything if it's more than you do now.

**5K, 4 runs**

- Tue: easy 30 minutes with 6 strides
- Thu: intervals, 5 × 3 minutes hard, 2 minutes jog
- Sat: easy 25 minutes
- Sun: long run, 50 minutes easy

**Half marathon, 4 runs**

- Tue: easy 40 minutes with 6 strides
- Thu: tempo, 20 minutes steady after a warm-up
- Sat: easy 30 minutes
- Sun: long run, 1 hour 30 easy

**Marathon, 5 runs**

- Tue: easy 45 minutes with strides
- Wed: threshold intervals, 4 × 8 minutes, 2 minutes jog
- Thu: easy 40 minutes
- Sat: easy 30 minutes
- Sun: long run, 2 hours 15 easy, practising fuel

Rest or cross-train on the other days. See <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/rest-days">rest days</TopicLink>.

## Choosing a plan

- **Start where you are.** The first week should look like what you already run. A plan that doubles your running in week one is too big a step. In new runners, weekly jumps of more than 30% went with more of some overuse injuries<Cite id="S5" />.
- **Fit your life.** A plan with four runs you'll actually do beats one with six you'll skip.
- **Allow slack.** Life happens. If you miss a week, don't try to make it up; carry on from where you are, or repeat a week.
- **Build in strength work.** Two short sessions a week. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">strength training</TopicLink>.

## The taper

In the last week or two before a big race, you cut back so you start fresh. Pooling studies of tapering, the biggest performance gains came from a taper of about two weeks in which training volume was cut by roughly 41 to 60%, while intensity and frequency were kept<Cite id="T4" />.

In practice, that means fewer and shorter runs, but still running most days you usually would, and keeping a little faster running in to stay sharp. Many runners feel restless or a bit sluggish during a taper, and that's fine. See <TopicLink to="racing/taper">taper and race week</TopicLink>.
