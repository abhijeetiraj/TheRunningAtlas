<Box kind="care">

**Call emergency services** if a young runner collapses. If they aren't breathing normally, start CPR and send for a defibrillator (AED).

**See a doctor before running again** after fainting or dizziness during or just after exercise, or chest pain, unusual breathlessness or palpitations with exercise<Cite id="D11" />.

**Stop running and get it checked soon** for pain in one spot on a bone, pain at rest or at night, a limp, or pain at the knee or heel that keeps coming back. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/when-to-get-checked">when to stop and get checked</TopicLink>.

</Box>

## Rest and variety

The American Academy of Pediatrics advises that young athletes take 1 to 2 days a week off their main sport, and at least 3 months a year off it, in blocks of about a month. That's to help them recover and to lower the risk of overuse injury and burnout. It also advises putting off specialising in one sport<Cite id="D12" />.

A break from running doesn't have to mean doing nothing. Other sports and active play can fill the gap.

## Building up

The same principles as for adults apply. Build up gradually, keep most running easy, and leave room for school, sleep and other sports. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/sleep">sleep</TopicLink>.

## Eating enough

Teens need energy for growing as well as for training. Low energy availability affects female and male athletes, and often goes unrecognised by athletes, coaches and clinicians<Cite id="D7" />. Periods that become irregular or stop, or a bone stress injury, are worth seeing a doctor about. See <TopicLink to="different-runners/red-s">low energy availability</TopicLink>.

<Box kind="note" title="For parents and coaches">

Keep it fun and varied, and let young runners have a say in how much they do. Ask how they feel, not only how fast they ran. Talk about food as fuel for growing and training, not about weight.

</Box>
