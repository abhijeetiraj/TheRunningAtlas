**See a doctor before you start** if you have pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw or arms; breathlessness at rest or with mild effort; dizziness or fainting; ankle swelling; palpitations; calf pain on walking; or unusual tiredness<Cite id="S25" />. If you have a heart, metabolic or kidney condition, such as diabetes, ask your doctor too. See <TopicLink to="start-running/see-a-doctor-first">see a doctor first?</TopicLink>

## Why it's worth it

Pooling 14 studies from six cohorts (232,149 people), any amount of running was linked with a lower risk of death from any cause than no running, including amounts under 50 minutes a week. That's a link, not proof of cause<Cite id="D15" />.

You don't need to change your body to start running, and running doesn't have to be about weight. It can be about fitness, mood, time outdoors, or a race you'd like to finish.

## Start gently

The studies below grouped runners by body mass index (BMI), which is a rough measure of body size. With that in mind:

- In 930 novice runners followed for a year, a BMI over 30 was one of several factors linked with a higher risk of running injury. A link, not proof of cause<Cite id="D13" />.
- In a 3-week study of 749 novice runners, those with a BMI over 30 who ran more than 3 km in their first week had a much higher injury risk. The authors suggested starting with less than 3 km of running in the first week, and called for larger trials to confirm it<Cite id="D14" />.

So the first few weeks are the time to be patient. Short run and walk sessions are the gentlest start. Beginner plans such as Couch to 5K begin with short runs between walks and build to about 30 minutes of running over nine weeks<Cite id="S1" />. It's fine to take longer, and to repeat a week. See <TopicLink to="start-running/first-run">your first run</TopicLink>, which has a run and walk planner.

## Keep it easy

- **Go at a pace you could talk at.** Walking breaks are part of the plan, not a failure. See <TopicLink to="start-running/talk-test">the talk test</TopicLink>.
- **Build up gradually**, and keep a day between runs at first. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.
- **Mix it up** with walking, cycling or swimming on other days if you like. See <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink>.

## Kit and comfort

- **Shoes**: choose a pair that feels comfortable and fits well. See <TopicLink to="start-running/shoes-and-kit">shoes and kit</TopicLink>.
- **Chafing**: fitted, moisture-wicking clothes and an anti-chafe balm on the inner thighs, underarms and anywhere that rubs can help.
- **Support**: a well-fitting sports bra if you need one.

## Soreness and pain

Some muscle soreness in the first weeks is common and fades. Pain in one spot, pain that gets worse as you run, or pain that changes how you run is different. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">soreness or pain?</TopicLink>
