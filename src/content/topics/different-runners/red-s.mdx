<Box kind="care">

**See a doctor** if your periods have become irregular or stopped, you've had a bone stress injury, especially more than once, or you've lost weight without meaning to.

**If you're worried about your eating**, or feel you have to run to make up for what you eat, talk to a doctor. You don't have to be underweight for it to matter, and help is available.

</Box>

## What it is

Energy availability is the energy left from what you eat after your training has used its share. That's what your body has for everything else: hormones, bone, immunity, repair and growth.

The International Olympic Committee describes low energy availability as a spectrum<Cite id="D7" />:

- **Brief or mild shortfalls** can be adaptable and largely harmless.
- **Large, long or frequent shortfalls** are a problem, and lead to relative energy deficiency in sport, now written REDs.

It affects female and male athletes, and it often goes unrecognised by athletes, coaches and clinicians<Cite id="D7" />. It can happen by accident, when training goes up and eating doesn't, as well as through deliberate restriction.

## What it can affect

The IOC lists effects including, but not limited to<Cite id="D8" />:

- **Menstrual function**: periods may become irregular or stop.
- **Bone health**. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/bone-stress">bone stress injuries</TopicLink>.
- **Immunity**.
- **Metabolic rate**.
- **Protein synthesis**, which matters for repair and adaptation.
- **Heart health**.

These are not a checklist for diagnosing yourself. They can have other causes, so if you notice them, get them checked.

## What helps

- **Eat enough for your training**, especially on bigger days and in heavier weeks. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/everyday-eating">everyday eating</TopicLink>.
- **Fuel around your runs**, particularly long or hard ones. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/before-during">before and during runs</TopicLink>.
- **Ask for help.** A doctor can look into it, and a sports dietitian can help you match your eating to your training.

<Box kind="note" title="For coaches and parents">

If you coach or parent a runner, ask about eating and periods kindly, and point towards a doctor rather than trying to sort it out yourself. See <TopicLink to="different-runners/teens">teen runners</TopicLink>.

</Box>
