## Pace and effort

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="pace">Pace</dt>
<dd>The time it takes to run one kilometre or one mile, such as 6:00 per km. See the <TopicLink to="tools/pace-calculator">pace calculator</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="speed">Speed</dt>
<dd>Pace the other way up: the distance you cover in an hour. 6:00 per km is 10 km/h.</dd>
<dt id="split">Split</dt>
<dd>Your time for one part of a run or race, such as each kilometre or each half.</dd>
<dt id="even-pace">Even pace and even effort</dt>
<dd>Running at the same pace all the way, or at the same effort, which on a hilly course means slower up and faster down. See <TopicLink to="racing/pacing">pacing</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="negative-split">Negative split</dt>
<dd>Running the second half of a race faster than the first.</dd>
<dt id="easy">Easy running</dt>
<dd>Running at a relaxed, conversational effort, where you could talk in full sentences. See the <TopicLink to="start-running/talk-test">talk test</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="cadence">Cadence</dt>
<dd>How many steps you take in a minute, counting both feet. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/cadence-step-length">cadence and step length</TopicLink> and the <TopicLink to="tools/cadence-counter">cadence counter</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="step-length">Step length</dt>
<dd>The distance from one foot landing to the other foot landing. Speed is cadence times step length.</dd>
<dt id="max-hr">Maximal heart rate</dt>
<dd>The highest your heart rate goes in an all-out effort. Formulas based on age only give a rough guess. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/heart-rate-zones">heart-rate zones</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="hrr">Heart rate reserve</dt>
<dd>Your maximal heart rate minus your resting heart rate. A share of it, added to your resting heart rate, gives a training target. See the <TopicLink to="tools/heart-rate-zones">heart-rate zones tool</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="zones">Zones</dt>
<dd>Bands of effort, set by heart rate, pace or feel. Different watches and plans draw the lines in different places, so "zone 2" doesn't always mean the same thing.</dd>
</dl>

## Your body

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="aerobic">Aerobic</dt>
<dd>Making energy with oxygen, inside your muscle cells. It's slower to get going but can keep going for hours. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/energy-systems">energy systems</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="anaerobic">Anaerobic</dt>
<dd>Making energy without needing oxygen. It's fast but can't be kept up for long, so it powers sprints and hard bursts.</dd>
<dt id="vo2max">VO2max</dt>
<dd>The most oxygen your body can use in a minute, usually given per kilogram of body mass. It's the size of your aerobic engine. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/vo2max">VO2max</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="economy">Running economy</dt>
<dd>How much oxygen you need to run at a given speed. A more economical runner uses less. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/running-economy">running economy</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="lactate">Lactate</dt>
<dd>A fuel your muscles make and use all the time, not a waste product. It builds up in the blood as you run harder. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/lactate-thresholds">lactate and thresholds</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="threshold">Threshold</dt>
<dd>An effort where something changes, such as lactate starting to build up in the blood or your breathing getting harder. Training plans often use thresholds to set paces.</dd>
<dt id="critical-speed">Critical speed</dt>
<dd>A threshold you can estimate from two races. Below it, effort can settle to a steady state; above it, effort keeps rising until you have to stop.</dd>
<dt id="glycogen">Glycogen</dt>
<dd>The form carbohydrate is stored in, in your muscles and liver. The store is small, so it can run low in long, hard efforts. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/carbs-and-fat">carbs and fat as fuel</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="wall">Hitting the wall</dt>
<dd>Running low on carbohydrate in a long race, when a pace you could hold suddenly feels impossible. See <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="drift">Cardiovascular drift</dt>
<dd>The slow rise in heart rate over a long, steady run, even at the same pace. Heat and dehydration can make it larger.</dd>
<dt id="fibres">Slow and fast fibres</dt>
<dd>The two main kinds of muscle fibre. Slow fibres (type I) tire very slowly; fast fibres (types IIa and IIx) are more powerful but tire sooner. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/muscle-fibres">muscle fibre types</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="acclimation">Heat acclimation</dt>
<dd>How your body adapts to exercising in the heat, with earlier and greater sweating and a lower heart rate. Much of it comes in the first week or so. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/heat-and-sweat">heat and sweat</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="leg-spring">Leg spring</dt>
<dd>The way your leg squashes as you land and springs back as you push off, with tendons such as the Achilles storing and returning energy. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/springs-and-tendons">springs and tendons</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="contact">Ground contact time</dt>
<dd>How long each foot stays on the ground in a step. Many watches estimate it, along with vertical oscillation, how much you bounce. See <TopicLink to="training/watch-metrics">what your watch metrics mean</TopicLink>.</dd>
</dl>

## Training

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="long-run">Long run</dt>
<dd>The longest run of your week, usually at an easy effort. It grows over a plan. See <TopicLink to="training/workouts">workouts explained</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="tempo">Tempo run</dt>
<dd>A run that is hard enough to need concentration but steady enough to hold for 20 minutes or more.</dd>
<dt id="intervals">Intervals</dt>
<dd>Repeated fast efforts of a few minutes, with easy running or rest in between.</dd>
<dt id="strides">Strides</dt>
<dd>Short, fast, relaxed runs of about 15 to 25 seconds, building up to near your fastest smooth pace, with a walk back between them.</dd>
<dt id="fartlek">Fartlek</dt>
<dd>"Speed play" in Swedish: an easy run with bursts of faster running when you feel like it.</dd>
<dt id="hill-repeats">Hill repeats</dt>
<dd>Running hard up a hill, then easing back down, several times. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/hills">uphill and downhill</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="run-walk">Run-walk</dt>
<dd>Mixing short runs with walking breaks, a common way to start running. See <TopicLink to="start-running/first-run">your first run</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="polarised">Polarised training</dt>
<dd>Training that is mostly easy, with some hard sessions and little in between. See <TopicLink to="training/intensity-mix">the intensity mix</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="cutback">Easier week</dt>
<dd>Also called a cutback or recovery week: a week with less running, every few weeks in a plan, to let your body catch up. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="taper">Taper</dt>
<dd>Cutting back your running in the last week or two before a race, so you start fresh. See <TopicLink to="racing/taper">taper and race week</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="cross-training">Cross-training</dt>
<dd>Other exercise, such as cycling, swimming or deep-water running, that keeps you fit with less pounding. See <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="carb-loading">Carbohydrate loading</dt>
<dd>Eating more carbohydrate than usual for a day or two before a long race, to top up your glycogen. See <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink>.</dd>
</dl>

## Shoes and kit

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="stack">Stack height</dt>
<dd>How thick the sole is, under the heel or the forefoot. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/shoe-science">shoe science</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="drop">Drop</dt>
<dd>The heel stack minus the forefoot stack. A shoe with an 8 mm drop has a heel 8 mm higher than the forefoot.</dd>
<dt id="plate">Carbon plate</dt>
<dd>A stiff plate built into the sole of many racing shoes, usually with a soft, bouncy foam.</dd>
</dl>

## Racing

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="pb">PB</dt>
<dd>Personal best: your fastest time for a distance.</dd>
<dt id="chip-time">Chip time and gun time</dt>
<dd>Gun time starts when the race starts. Chip time starts when you cross the start line, so in a big race it's often shorter.</dd>
<dt id="aid-station">Aid station</dt>
<dd>A point on the course with water, and often sports drink or gels.</dd>
<dt id="half">Half marathon and marathon</dt>
<dd>A marathon is 42.195 km (26.2 miles). A half is half that, about 21.1 km (13.1 miles). See <TopicLink to="racing/half-or-full">half or full?</TopicLink></dd>
</dl>

## Injuries and health

<Box kind="care" title="Definitions only">

These definitions can't tell you what's wrong. If you're worried about pain or illness, see a doctor or physiotherapist. For confusion, collapse, a seizure or chest pain, **call emergency services**.

</Box>

<dl class="gloss">
<dt id="doms">DOMS</dt>
<dd>Delayed onset muscle soreness: the spread-out muscle ache that starts within a day or so of unaccustomed exercise. See <TopicLink to="start-running/soreness">soreness: what is normal</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="runners-knee">Runner's knee</dt>
<dd>Patellofemoral pain: an ache around or behind the kneecap. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/runners-knee">runner's knee</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="shin-splints">Shin splints</dt>
<dd>Medial tibial stress syndrome: pain along the inner edge of the shin bone. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/shin-pain">shin pain</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="bone-stress">Bone stress injury</dt>
<dd>Bone that is failing to keep up with repeated loading, from an early stress reaction to a stress fracture. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/bone-stress">bone stress injuries</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="achilles">Achilles tendinopathy</dt>
<dd>Pain and stiffness in the Achilles tendon, at the back of the ankle. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/achilles-pain">Achilles pain</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="plantar">Plantar heel pain</dt>
<dd>Often called plantar fasciitis: pain under the heel or in the arch. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/heel-pain">heel pain</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="itb">ITB syndrome</dt>
<dd>Iliotibial band syndrome: pain on the outside of the knee, where a long band of tissue down the outside of the thigh crosses it. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/outer-knee-itb">outer knee pain</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="hyponatraemia">Exercise-associated hyponatraemia</dt>
<dd>Low blood sodium during or after exercise. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/fluids-sodium">fluids and sodium</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="rhabdo">Exertional rhabdomyolysis</dt>
<dd>Serious muscle breakdown after exercise, which releases the muscle's contents into the blood. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/red-flags">red flags</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="heat-illness">Heat exhaustion and heat stroke</dt>
<dd>Illness from overheating. Heat stroke, with confusion, odd behaviour or collapse, is a medical emergency: call emergency services. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/red-flags">red flags</TopicLink>.</dd>
<dt id="red-s">RED-S</dt>
<dd>Relative energy deficiency in sport: problems with how the body works caused by eating too little for the energy you use. See <TopicLink to="different-runners/red-s">low energy availability</TopicLink>.</dd>
</dl>
