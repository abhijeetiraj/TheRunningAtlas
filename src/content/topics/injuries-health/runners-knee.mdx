**Seek care now** if you can't put weight on the leg, or the knee swelled up quickly after a fall, twist or knock.

**Get it checked soon** if the knee swells, locks, catches or gives way, if the pain is in one spot on a bone, or if it isn't improving after a few weeks of easing back.

</Box>

## What it is

Patellofemoral pain is a diffuse ache at the front of the knee, around or behind the kneecap. It usually comes on with activities that load the bent knee: squatting, running, and going up or down stairs. Pain on squatting is the most useful sign a clinician looks for<Cite id="I5" />.

Pooling studies, it was among the most common injuries in ultramarathon runners<Cite id="I1" />.

It's often thought of as an overload problem, with the knee asked to do more than it's ready for, but its exact causes are still debated.

## What helps

An international consensus of researchers and clinicians made these recommendations<Cite id="I6" />:

- **Exercise therapy** is the main treatment, to reduce pain in the short, medium and long term and improve function.
- **Hip and knee exercise together** is preferred over knee exercise alone.
- **Foot orthoses (insoles)** and **patellar taping** may help in the short term.

The <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">Strength library</TopicLink> shows exercises that work the thigh and hip muscles, such as squats, step-ups, split squats and the hip hike. A physiotherapist can choose the right ones for you and set how deep and how heavy to go, so that the exercises don't flare your pain.

## Running while it settles

You may not have to stop running completely. A physiotherapist can help you find a level that doesn't make the pain worse:

- **Run less**, and try flatter routes if hills seem to make it worse.
- **Use the pain scale**: pain during and after the run stays mild, has settled by the next morning, and doesn't creep up from week to week. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">soreness or pain?</TopicLink>
- **Try a slightly higher step rate** at the same speed. A small increase reduced loading at the knee and hip<Cite id="R8" />. See <TopicLink to="how-running-works/cadence-step-length">cadence and step length</TopicLink>.
- **Build back gradually** once it settles. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/coming-back">coming back after injury</TopicLink>.
