## Shin splints

Medial tibial stress syndrome, usually called shin splints, is pain along the inner edge of the shin bone (the tibia), usually in its lower half. It comes on with running, and the bone is tender to press along a length of it, not just in one spot.

Pooling studies of runners, it was among the most common running injuries, affecting about 14 to 20% of runners during follow-up<Cite id="I1" />.

## Who gets it

Pooling studies of runners, factors linked with shin splints included being female, having had it before, a higher body mass index, a larger drop of the arch, having used orthotics before, and fewer years of running<Cite id="I8" />. These are links, not proof of cause, and several of them can't be changed.

One thing you can control is how fast you build up. New runners have higher injury rates than experienced ones<Cite id="S6" />, and big jumps in weekly distance have been linked with more overuse injuries<Cite id="S5" />. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.

## What helps

Reviewing 11 trials, none of the treatments tested, including laser, stretching and strengthening, compression stockings, braces and electromagnetic fields, was proven effective. All the trials had a high risk of bias<Cite id="I7" />. That means the evidence is too weak to say they work, not that they've been shown not to.

In practice, the approach is:

1. **Reduce the load** to a level that doesn't make the pain worse. That may mean running less, or swapping some runs for <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink> for a while.
2. **Build back gradually** once you can run without pain. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/coming-back">coming back after injury</TopicLink>.
3. **Keep up general strength work.** Many physiotherapists include calf and hip exercises, though, as above, strengthening hasn't been proven to treat shin splints. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">strength training for runners</TopicLink>.

Shin splints can be frustrating and slow to settle. If it isn't improving after a few weeks of reduced running, see a physiotherapist or sports doctor.

## Shin splints or a bone stress injury?

The two can feel similar. A bone stress injury is bone that is failing to keep up with repeated loading, and it causes localised pain and tenderness<Cite id="I13" />. Pain in one small spot, or pain at rest or at night, makes a clinician think of a bone stress injury. Only an examination, and sometimes a scan, can tell them apart<Cite id="I14" />.
