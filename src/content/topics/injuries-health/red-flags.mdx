<Box kind="care" title="Call emergency services">

**Call emergency services** for any one of these, in yourself or another runner: chest pain or pressure; collapse; confusion or odd behaviour; a seizure; severe breathlessness that doesn't settle with rest.

**If someone collapses and isn't breathing normally**, call emergency services, start CPR straight away, and send someone for a defibrillator (AED). The operator can talk you through it.

</Box>

This page is about recognising an emergency and getting help, not about telling conditions apart. Several of these look alike, and clinicians sort them out with an examination and tests.

## Chest pain and collapse

**Stop running and call emergency services** if you have chest pain, pressure or tightness, or pain spreading to the arm, neck or jaw. Don't try to run through it, and don't wait to see if it passes.

Some symptoms call for a medical check before you carry on training, even if they settle: pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw or arms; breathlessness at rest or with mild effort; dizziness or fainting; palpitations; and unusual tiredness<Cite id="S25" />. See <TopicLink to="start-running/see-a-doctor-first">see a doctor first?</TopicLink>

When a runner's heart stops, what bystanders do makes a big difference. Among 10.9 million runners in US marathons and half marathons from 2000 to 2010, 59 had a cardiac arrest during the race or within an hour of finishing. Most were men running marathons. Bystander CPR was the strongest predictor of survival<Cite id="I16" />.

<Box kind="try" title="Learn CPR">

A short CPR course teaches you what to do if someone collapses, on a run or anywhere else. Find one through a local first-aid organisation, and look out for where the defibrillators are on your usual routes and at races.

</Box>

## Heat illness

Running in the heat, especially before you're used to it, raises the risk of heat illness<Cite id="E44" />.

**Heat exhaustion** can bring feeling faint or dizzy, weakness, headache, nausea or vomiting<Cite id="E44" />. If you have any of these, stop running, get out of the heat, start cooling down, and **seek care now**.

**Heat stroke** is a medical emergency. Its signs include confusion or other changes in behaviour with a very high body temperature<Cite id="E33" />. **Call emergency services.** Cooling quickly is the priority: medical teams cool the person in cold water straight away<Cite id="E33" />. While you wait for help, move them into the shade and cool them with whatever you have, such as cold water, ice or wet towels.

See <TopicLink to="the-engine/heat-and-sweat">heat and sweat</TopicLink>.

## Rhabdomyolysis

Very hard or unaccustomed exercise can break down so much muscle that its contents leak into the blood and can harm the kidneys. This is exertional rhabdomyolysis.

Its warning signs include muscle pain or weakness out of proportion to the exercise, marked swelling, and tea- or cola-coloured urine<Cite id="S21" />. Any one of these means **seek care now**. Confusion means **call emergency services**.

It's diagnosed by clinicians from your symptoms together with blood tests, which may need repeating, and the treatment depends on how severe it is. Heat and being dehydrated can make it worse. Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether your medicines or supplements change your risk. See <TopicLink to="start-running/soreness">soreness</TopicLink>.

## Low blood sodium

Exercise-associated hyponatraemia is low blood sodium during or after exercise. It's mostly caused by drinking more than you lose<Cite id="E45" />.

It may cause no symptoms or mild ones, such as light-headedness, dizziness, nausea, puffiness and weight gain during the event. Severe cases bring vomiting, headache, confusion or agitation, seizures and coma<Cite id="F18" />. Confusion, a seizure or collapse means **call emergency services**.

Its symptoms overlap with other causes of collapse, including heat illness, so a blood test is needed to tell them apart<Cite id="F18" />. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/fluids-sodium">fluids and sodium</TopicLink>.

## Other signs that need care now

- **A calf that is painful, swollen, warm or red**: seek care now.
- **An asthma attack** that your reliever isn't helping: call emergency services. See <TopicLink to="different-runners/asthma-diabetes">running with asthma or diabetes</TopicLink>.
- **Low blood sugar** with confusion, or a person who can't safely swallow or is unconscious: call emergency services.
