<Box kind="care">

**Seek care now** if you can't put weight on the leg, or the knee swelled up quickly after a fall, twist or knock.

**Get it checked soon** if the knee swells, locks, catches or gives way, if you have numbness or tingling, or if it isn't improving after a few weeks of easing back. Outer knee pain has other causes, and an examination can sort them out.

</Box>

## What it is

The iliotibial band, or ITB, is a long, thick band of tissue down the outside of the thigh, from the hip to just below the knee. Iliotibial band syndrome is pain on the outside of the knee, where the band crosses it, brought on by running.

A review of the research in runners found that the hip, knee and foot appear to move differently in runners with the condition. It's not clear whether that's a cause or an effect<Cite id="I12" />.

## What the research shows

The same review found that research on treating it was of poor quality, and its results conflicted<Cite id="I12" />. So there's no treatment that can be recommended with confidence. That means the evidence is too weak to say what works, not that nothing does.

## What to do

With no proven treatment, the usual approach is the same as for other overuse pain:

1. **Ease the load** to a level that doesn't make the pain worse. That may mean running less, or swapping some runs for <TopicLink to="training/cross-training">cross-training</TopicLink>.
2. **Use the pain scale** while you keep running: pain stays mild, has settled by the next morning, and doesn't creep up from week to week. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">soreness or pain?</TopicLink>
3. **Keep up hip and thigh strength work.** The <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/strength-training">Strength library</TopicLink> includes the hip hike and the glute bridge. A physiotherapist can choose and adjust exercises for you.
4. **Build back gradually** once it settles. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/coming-back">coming back after injury</TopicLink>.

Many runners foam roll the outside of the thigh. See <TopicLink to="strength-mobility-drills/foam-rolling">foam rolling</TopicLink> for what the research says about rolling in general.
