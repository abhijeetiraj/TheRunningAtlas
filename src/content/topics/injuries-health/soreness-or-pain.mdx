<Box kind="care" title="Stop and seek care now">

**Stop running and seek care now** if you have any one of these: sudden severe pain; a snap or pop; you can't put weight on the leg; a calf that is painful, swollen, warm or red; muscle pain or weakness far beyond what the run should cause; marked swelling of a muscle; or tea- or cola-coloured urine<Cite id="S21" />.

**Call emergency services** for chest pain, collapse, confusion or a seizure. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/red-flags">red flags</TopicLink>.

</Box>

## Normal soreness

After a run that's longer, faster or hillier than usual, or a new strength session, your muscles can ache for a few days. This delayed muscle soreness usually starts within a day, peaks around one to three days, and fades within about a week<Cite id="I3" />.

It tends to be:

- **In the muscles**, such as the thighs or calves, not in a joint, tendon or bone.
- **Spread out**, not in one spot.
- **On both sides**, if both legs did the same work.

It's a common response to unaccustomed work, and repeating the same session causes less soreness next time<Cite id="H25" />. See <TopicLink to="start-running/soreness">soreness</TopicLink>.

## Signs of an injury

Pain is more likely to be an injury if it:

- Is in **one spot**, or in a joint, tendon or bone.
- Is on **one side** only.
- **Gets worse as you run**, rather than easing.
- **Changes how you run**, so you limp or shorten your stride.
- Is there **at rest or at night**, or wakes you.
- Comes with **swelling**, bruising, locking or giving way.
- Is **still there the next day**, or worse each week.

Any one of these is a reason to stop running for now and get it checked by a physiotherapist or doctor. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/when-to-get-checked">when to stop and get checked</TopicLink>.

## Running with mild pain

Not every ache means stopping completely. For some overuse problems, physiotherapists use a pain-monitoring model. In a trial of 38 people with Achilles tendon pain, those who kept running and jumping within these limits did as well as those who rested from them<Cite id="I2" />:

- Pain during and after running stays at **5 or below** on a scale of 0 (none) to 10 (worst imaginable).
- It has **settled by the next morning**.
- It **doesn't creep up** from week to week.

This was tested for Achilles pain, not every injury. It's not for bone pain, sudden injuries or anything on the warning list above. Agree it with a physiotherapist if you can.

## The common running injuries

Pooling studies of about 3,500 runners, the most common running injuries were shin splints, Achilles tendon pain and plantar heel pain. In ultramarathon runners, Achilles pain and pain at the front of the knee were most common<Cite id="I1" />.
