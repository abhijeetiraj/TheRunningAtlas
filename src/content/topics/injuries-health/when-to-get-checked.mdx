<Box kind="care" title="Call emergency services">

**Call emergency services** for chest pain or pressure, collapse, confusion or odd behaviour, a seizure, or severe breathlessness that doesn't settle with rest. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/red-flags">red flags</TopicLink>.

</Box>

These lists are not a full set of symptoms, and they can't tell you what's wrong. If you're worried about something that isn't here, get it checked.

## Seek care now

Stop running and **seek care now** if you have any one of these:

- **Sudden severe pain**, or a **snap or pop** you felt or heard.
- You **can't put weight** on the leg.
- A joint that **swelled up quickly** after a fall, twist or knock.
- A **calf that is painful, swollen, warm or red**.
- **Muscle pain or weakness far beyond** what the run should cause, **marked swelling** of a muscle, or **tea- or cola-coloured urine**<Cite id="S21" />.
- **Pain deep in the groin** or at the front of the hip that comes on with running or walking<Cite id="I14" />.

## Get it checked soon

Stop running and **get it checked soon** by a physiotherapist or doctor if you have:

- Pain in **one small spot on a bone** that hurts to press.
- Pain **at rest or at night**, or pain that wakes you.
- A **limp**, or pain that changes how you run.
- **Swelling**, **locking**, catching or **giving way** in a joint.
- **Numbness or tingling**.
- Pain that **isn't improving** after a few weeks of easing back.

Some symptoms during exercise call for a medical check before you carry on training, such as feeling dizzy or faint, palpitations, unusual breathlessness, or calf pain on walking<Cite id="S25" />. See <TopicLink to="start-running/see-a-doctor-first">see a doctor first?</TopicLink>

## When you may keep running

For some overuse aches, physiotherapists use a pain limit rather than full rest. In a trial in Achilles tendon pain, running within these limits did as well as resting<Cite id="I2" />:

- Pain during and after running stays at **5 or below out of 10**.
- It has **settled by the next morning**.
- It **doesn't creep up** from week to week.

It's not for anything on the lists above, and it was tested for Achilles pain, not every injury. Agree it with a physiotherapist if you can. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">soreness or pain?</TopicLink>

## Who to see

- **A physiotherapist** for most running aches and overuse injuries. In many places you can book one directly.
- **A GP or family doctor** if you're unsure where to start, for symptoms that aren't from muscles or joints, or for a referral.
- **A sports medicine doctor** for injuries that aren't settling, possible bone stress injuries, or when a scan may be needed.

## What to tell them

Write it down before you go. It makes the appointment more useful:

- **Where** it hurts, and whether it's one spot or spread out.
- **When** it started, and whether it came on suddenly or gradually.
- **What makes it worse** or better: running, stairs, mornings, rest.
- **What changed** in the weeks before: more distance, more speed, hills, new shoes, a race.
- **Anything else**: past injuries, changes to your periods, illness, medicines, how you're eating.
