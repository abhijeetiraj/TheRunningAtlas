**Stop running and seek care now** if you have pain deep in the groin or at the front of the hip that comes on with running or walking, or pain anywhere that makes you limp. The neck of the thigh bone, near the hip, is a high-risk site for a bone stress injury<Cite id="I14" />.

**Stop running and get it checked soon** if the pain is in one small spot on a bone that hurts to press, if it hurts at rest or at night, or if it's on the top of the foot. Don't test it by running or hopping on it.

</Box>

## What it is

Bone is living tissue. It responds to loading by remodelling and getting stronger, but that takes time. A bone stress injury is bone that is failing to keep up with repeated loading, causing localised bone pain and tenderness<Cite id="I13" />.

It sits on a scale<Cite id="I13" />:

1. **Stress reaction**: the bone is under strain but hasn't cracked.
2. **Stress fracture**: a small crack has formed.
3. **Complete fracture**: the bone breaks through, if loading carries on.

Catching it early, before it moves along that scale, is the point of stopping when you notice the signs.

## Low-risk and high-risk sites

Where it happens changes the plan<Cite id="I14" />:

- **Low-risk sites** generally heal with reduced activity, followed by a gradual increase in load.
- **High-risk sites**, such as the neck of the thigh bone (femoral neck) and the navicular, a bone in the middle of the foot, may need time off weight-bearing, medicines or surgery. They need specialist care.

The <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">body map</TopicLink> marks a few places where bone stress injuries are felt. It can't tell you which kind you have.

## How it's diagnosed

A clinician diagnoses it from your history and an examination, and uses imaging to confirm it<Cite id="I14" />. Shin splints can feel similar, which is another reason to get it examined. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/shin-pain">shin pain</TopicLink>.

## Why it happens

Load that rises faster than bone can adapt is the usual story. In novice runners, big jumps in weekly distance were linked with more overuse injuries than small ones<Cite id="S5" />. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.

Energy matters too. Eating too little for the energy you use can affect bone health and periods, in men as well as women<Cite id="I15" />. If you've had a bone stress injury, especially more than one, or your periods have become irregular or stopped, it's worth raising with a doctor. See <TopicLink to="different-runners/red-s">low energy availability</TopicLink>.

## Coming back

A clinician will set the plan, and the timeline depends on the site. Expect a period of reduced load, then a gradual return. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/coming-back">coming back after injury</TopicLink>.
