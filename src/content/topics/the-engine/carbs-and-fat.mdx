## Two fuels

Your muscles' aerobic engine can burn two main fuels<Cite id="E1" />:

- **Fat**, stored under the skin, around the organs and in small droplets inside muscle fibres. Each gram holds about 9 kcal<Cite id="E29" />, and even lean people carry a lot of it.
- **Carbohydrate**, stored as glycogen in your muscles and liver, plus a little glucose in your blood. Each gram holds about 4 kcal<Cite id="E29" />, and the store is small.

Only carbohydrate can also be used without oxygen, for fast, hard efforts. See <TopicLink to="the-engine/energy-systems">energy systems</TopicLink>.

## What a kilometre costs

Running on the flat costs about 1 kcal for every kilogram of body mass, for every kilometre, and this changes little with speed<Cite id="E28" />. So a 70 kg runner uses roughly 70 kcal a kilometre, walking-pace jog or race pace. Running faster burns energy faster per minute, but each kilometre costs about the same.

## The crossover: from fat to carbohydrate

The mix you burn depends mainly on how hard you are running. As exercise gets harder, the body shifts from burning mostly fat towards burning mostly carbohydrate. This is called the crossover concept<Cite id="E25" />.

In one study of trained people tested at three intensities, fat supplied the largest share at moderate effort. At the hardest effort, muscle glycogen became the main fuel<Cite id="E26" />.

So on an easy run, a good share of your energy comes from fat. In a hard half marathon or marathon, most of it comes from carbohydrate.

## Glycogen and endurance

Because the glycogen store is small, it can run low in long, hard efforts. In a classic study, a diet high in carbohydrate raised muscle glycogen and let people exercise for longer at a fixed workload than a diet low in carbohydrate<Cite id="E27" />.

Runners call running low on carbohydrate in a long race "hitting the wall": the pace you could hold suddenly feels impossible. It's why marathon runners fuel before and during the race. See <TopicLink to="fuel-recovery/before-during">before and during runs</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink>.
