## What cadence is

Cadence is how many steps you take in a minute, counting both feet. Your speed is your cadence times the length of each step, so there are two ways to run faster: quicker steps, longer steps, or both. As runners speed up, both usually rise. The 3D runner in <TopicLink to="how-running-works/stride">the stride lab</TopicLink> follows a simple model of this<Cite id="R1" />.

Step length is speed divided by steps per second<Cite id="P2" />. The calculator above does it for you.

## Is there a right number?

You may have heard that everyone should run at 180 steps a minute. That number comes from coach Jack Daniels counting elite runners' steps at the 1984 Olympics<Cite id="P1" />. They were racing, at high speed. It was never a finding about what suits every runner at every pace.

Cadence depends on your speed, your legs and how you run. Two healthy runners at the same pace can have quite different cadences.

## When changing it can help

Taking slightly quicker, shorter steps at the same speed spreads the load differently. In one lab study, raising step rate by 5 to 10% reduced loading at the knee and hip<Cite id="R8" />. Some physiotherapists use this with runners who have knee pain.

If you try it, use the metronome at about 5% above your normal cadence for part of an easy run, and see how it feels. Change one thing at a time, and give it a few weeks.

<Box kind="note" title="Counting tips">

- Count on a flat stretch at your usual easy pace, after you have warmed up.
- With the 30-second count, count every time either foot lands, then the counter doubles it.
- Many watches estimate cadence too. Counting by hand once is a good check on what yours says.
