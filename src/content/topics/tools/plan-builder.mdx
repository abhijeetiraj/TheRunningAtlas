## How it works

The builder follows a few simple rules<Cite id="P6" />:

- **Week one is where you are now.** It doesn't jump you into a bigger week.
- **Weekly distance rises by at most about 10% a week**, up to a ceiling for each race that only stops the numbers growing without limit.
- **Every fourth week is easier**, at about three quarters of your biggest week so far.
- **The long run grows by about a kilometre a week**, or 10% once it's longer, up to a cap for each race, and to no more than about half your week.
- **The last week or two are a taper.** For a half marathon or marathon, the last two weeks are about 30% and then 50% below your biggest week. For a 5K or 10K, race week is about 40% below. Race week's distance doesn't include the race.

The typical plan lengths and longest runs it compares against are the coaching ranges on <TopicLink to="training/plans">plans from 5K to marathon</TopicLink>. They're illustrations, not tested prescriptions<Cite id="T8" />. For example, starting from 20 km a week with a longest run of 8 km, a 12-week half marathon outline peaks at about 39 km a week, with a longest run of about 16 km.

## Why these rules

- **Small steps.** In a trial with 532 novice runners, a programme built on the 10% rule had about as many injuries as a standard one<Cite id="S4" />, so 10% isn't a magic number. But in 873 novice runners, weekly jumps of more than 30% went with more of some overuse injuries: a link, not proof<Cite id="S5" />. See <TopicLink to="training/building-up">building up gradually</TopicLink>.
- **A taper.** Pooling studies, the biggest gains came from a taper of about two weeks that cut training volume by roughly 41 to 60%, while keeping intensity and how often you run<Cite id="T4" />. See <TopicLink to="racing/taper">taper and race week</TopicLink>.

## What it leaves to you

The outline gives distances, not sessions. Inside each week:

- **Keep most of your running easy**, with one or two harder sessions. See <TopicLink to="training/intensity-mix">the intensity mix</TopicLink> and <TopicLink to="training/workouts">workouts explained</TopicLink>.
- **Spread the distance over runs you'll actually do.** See the example weeks on <TopicLink to="training/plans">plans from 5K to marathon</TopicLink>.
- **Adjust when you need to.** If a week feels too much, repeat the one before. If you miss a week, carry on from where you are rather than catching up.
- **If something hurts**, don't push through the plan. See <TopicLink to="injuries-health/soreness-or-pain">soreness or pain?</TopicLink>
