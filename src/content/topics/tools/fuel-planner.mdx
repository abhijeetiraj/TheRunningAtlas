## How it works

The planner starts from the suggested carbohydrate for a race of your length<Cite id="F4" />:

| Race length | Suggested carbohydrate |
| --- | --- |
| Under an hour | Little or none |
| 1 to 2 hours | Up to about 30 g an hour |
| 2 to 2.5 hours | Up to about 60 g an hour |
| Beyond about 2.5 hours | Up to about 90 g an hour |

These are upper guides, not targets you must hit. The slider starts at the guide for your time, or at 60 g an hour if the guide is higher, because more than about 60 g an hour needs a mix of glucose and fructose<Cite id="F4" />. You can move it either way.

From your rate and the carbohydrate in one serving, it works out how often to take one. The first comes at 30 minutes, or sooner if you need them more often, and the last at least 15 minutes before you expect to finish. The distance for each one assumes an even pace<Cite id="P7" />.

For example, for a 4:15 marathon at 60 g an hour with 25 g gels, that's one gel every 25 minutes: nine gels, 225 g in all. The total comes out a bit under 60 g for every hour, because nothing is taken at the very start or end.

## Practise it first

- **Train your gut.** A diet high in carbohydrate raises the gut's capacity to absorb it, and practising eating and drinking during training may reduce stomach problems, though few studies have tested this directly<Cite id="F5" />. Try your plan, with the same products, on your longer runs.
- **Read the label.** Check the carbohydrate per serving, whether it has glucose and fructose, and whether it needs water.
- **Line up with aid stations.** Move each serving to the nearest drink station if you need water with it.
- **Drink to thirst.** The planner doesn't tell you how much to drink. Drinking more than you lose, over hours, is the main cause of low blood sodium in races<Cite id="C4" />. See <TopicLink to="racing/race-day-fuel">race-day fuel</TopicLink>.
