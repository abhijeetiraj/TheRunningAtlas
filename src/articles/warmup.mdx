## Why this routine

Long static holds before a run do not seem to prevent injuries. In a trial of more than 1,500 army recruits, adding short static stretches to the warm-up made no meaningful difference to injury risk<Cite id="W1" />. A trial of more than 2,700 runners, reported at a 2011 orthopaedic meeting rather than in a journal, found the same for a 3 to 5 minute stretch<Cite id="W2" />.

Long holds can also cost a little power. Holding a stretch for a minute or more per muscle, just before testing, dulled performance more than shorter holds, while dynamic stretching gave small gains<Cite id="W3" />. Warming up itself leaned towards fewer injuries in a review of five trials, but the evidence was not conclusive<Cite id="W4" />.

## The bigger levers

In a pooled analysis of 25 trials across many sports, stretching programmes showed no clear protective effect, while strength training programmes were linked with the biggest drop in injuries<Cite id="W5" />. Build your running gradually, and if you change your warm-up, change it slowly: in the trial of runners, those who switched routines had more injuries. That is a link, not proof<Cite id="W2" />.

## After the run

Static stretching fits better after a run, or as its own session if you want more flexibility. Hold 20 to 30 seconds at gentle tension, never into pain.
